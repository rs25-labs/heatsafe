How to use HeatSafe

HeatSafe compares walking routes by how much of each one is in shade at the time you'll walk, then recommends the safest one. Here's how to find your way around, and what we'd love to hear from you.

← Back to HeatSafe

Your first try (about 2 minutes)

  1. In From, choose Orange County School of the Arts. In To, choose Santa Ana Regional Transportation Center.

  2. Click Use demo day. That sets the trip to Thursday, Sep 10, 3:30 PM, a real 93°F afternoon. Then click Show me.

  3. Look at the map. Each route shows where you'd be in shade (navy) and where you'd be in direct sun (red). Hover over a route (or tap it on a phone) to see its time and shade, and click one to choose it. The small round icons are water fountains, parks, libraries and stores along the way.

  4. Compare the route cards on the left. Each shows the walking time, % shaded, minutes in direct sun, and a heat rating: Low, Moderate, High or Extreme.

  5. Drag the Leaving at bar at the bottom of the map. The shade on the map and the numbers on the cards change as you go later in the afternoon.

  6. Click Walk the shadiest route. On a laptop you'll see a 3D view following the walk, with a small map of the whole route in the corner. Use Pause, drag the slider, or try the 10× / 20× / 40× speeds. Click Done to go back.

    The 3D city takes 10 to 20 seconds to load the first time.

  7. Click Español at the top right to see the app in Spanish.

Then try a walk of your own

  • Click Edit trip, then pick places with Pick on the map or Search an address.
  • The app covers central Santa Ana only: downtown and the neighborhoods around it. If you pick a place outside that area, the app says so.
  • Leave Now, or choose At a set time to try a hot afternoon.
  • Add a note like "carrying groceries" or a bus time under Need to catch a bus or train.

Good to know

  • The first visit after a quiet spell can take up to 20 seconds while the server wakes up. You'll see "Waking up the server…". After that it's quick.
  • Ratings are estimates from real weather, building and tree heights, and the sun's position. They aren't measurements.
  • The 3D view only appears on laptops and tablets. On a phone, the map follows the walk instead.

What we'd love to hear

  • Was it clear what to do first? Where did you get stuck or confused?
  • Did the recommended route make sense to you? Would you actually walk it?
  • Was anything on the map or the cards hard to read or understand?
  • How did the walk playback feel: too fast, too slow, choppy?
  • What device and browser did you use? If something broke, a screenshot helps a lot.
